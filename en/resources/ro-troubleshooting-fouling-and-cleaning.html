RO Troubleshooting: Diagnose Fouling Before Cleaning

Low permeate flow, rising differential pressure or changing salt passage are symptoms, not diagnoses. First verify instruments and sampling, compare normalized performance with a valid baseline, review feed and pretreatment changes, then identify the likely foulant. Clean only under the current membrane and system cleaning guide with approved chemistry and safety controls.

Symptom triage

Low flow may reflect lower temperature, pressure or feed flow, changed recovery, fouling, scaling, instrumentation error or increased permeate backpressure. High stage differential pressure can indicate feed-channel blockage, solids, biofouling or hydraulic imbalance. Rising salt passage may reflect feed/temperature changes, oxidation, seal leakage, damage or measurement error. These causes overlap; do not infer a single cause from one indicator.

A practical evidence set includes normalized permeate flow and salt passage, pressure by stage, feed/concentrate/permeate flows and conductivity, temperature, pH, recovery, SDI/turbidity, pretreatment chemical records, alarms, cleaning history and representative feed/concentrate chemistry. Check calibration and compare equivalent operating conditions before attributing change to membrane fouling.

Fouling clues and limits

The presentation’s page 62 labels a breakdown of membrane failure cases as 4% mechanical, 18% degradation, and 78% fouling. It gives no denominator, sample definition, method, period, or current applicability; these figures are only an unverified historical supplier sample, not a failure probability or general risk estimate.

The source material illustrates particulate, mineral scale, biological, colloidal/organic and iron-related fouling. Visual appearance or odor can suggest a path for investigation but cannot establish chemical identity or a safe cleaning recipe. Laboratory membrane autopsy can add evidence; the sample must be handled and interpreted by qualified personnel.

Cleaning threshold discrepancy

The supplied presentation lists normalized permeate decline above 15%, salt-passage increase above 10% or feed-to-concentrate pressure-drop increase above 15% as cleaning indicators. Current published quality terms state that a 10% system-performance decrease under standard conditions calls for cleaning according to the specified procedure. This statement does not define a universal normalization method or replace the current model-specific cleaning guide. These criteria differ and should not be combined. Use the current exact-model cleaning guide and system protocol; confirm what “performance decrease” means and how normalization is calculated.

Chemical cleaning boundary

The source presentation contains concentrations, pH and temperature for acid, alkali, chelant and surfactant cleaning examples. This page intentionally does not reproduce these as instructions: compatibility depends on membrane model, foulant, mixing order, temperature, pH, system materials and current guide. Incorrect chemistry can permanently damage elements or expose workers to hazards. Do not clean from a generic table or this article.

Frequently asked questions

Should I clean when flow drops by a fixed percentage?

Follow the current model-specific guide and plant protocol; compare normalized data and verify instruments first.

Does high differential pressure prove biofouling?

No. Solids, scale, hydraulic changes and measurement faults can produce similar symptoms.

Can I use the source presentation’s recipe table?

No. It is legacy supplier material and not a current universal instruction.
