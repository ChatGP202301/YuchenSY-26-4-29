RO and NF Membrane Principles: Rejection, Flux, Recovery and Osmotic Pressure

RO and NF performance cannot be judged from TDS or a single rejection percentage. Interpret rejection, salt passage, permeate flow, flux, recovery, temperature, pressure, feed chemistry and the membrane’s stated test conditions together. A product-element rating is not a guarantee of full-system permeate quality.

Core definitions

Observed salt rejection is commonly expressed as (1 − permeate concentration ÷ feed concentration) × 100%. Salt passage is 100% minus rejection. Flux is permeate flow divided by active membrane area. Recovery is permeate flow divided by feed flow. Report the sampling location, concentration method, operating conditions and whether values are normalized.

An educational osmotic-pressure approximation is π = RTΣCi for dilute ideal solutions, with consistent units and the dissolved-species convention made explicit. A source example uses 2 g/L NaCl at 25°C: C≈0.0342 mol/L and π≈169 kPa (1.69 bar). This is an approximation, not a full seawater activity model.

A source table mislabeled NaCl 34.2 and MgSO₄ 16.7 as mol/L. From its own molecular weights and 2,000 mg/L inputs, the consistent units are mmol/L (0.0342 and 0.0167 mol/L). This correction is explicit; the printed unit must not be repeated.

Why operating conditions matter

Higher effective pressure can raise water flux; temperature changes viscosity and salt transport; feed salinity raises osmotic pressure; pH and ionic composition can affect membrane and scaling behavior. Concentration polarization means membrane-surface concentration may exceed bulk-feed concentration, so apparent and intrinsic rejection differ. Effects depend on membrane type, element configuration, recovery and test method.

A supplier example lists LP22-8040 with 99.7% stable rejection, 10,500 GPD and 400 ft², giving 26.25 GFD by division. The published product specification identifies these as model-specific ratings; compare only under the stated test conditions. Element ratings do not predict full-system permeate quality or guarantee plant performance.

What to send for a useful review

Provide a recent full water analysis, feed and target permeate quality, required flow, temperature range, pH, recovery target, operating pressure, pretreatment, recovery/staging and current normalized performance if replacing elements. TDS alone cannot reveal scaling ions, silica, organics, oxidants or fouling risk.

Frequently asked questions

Does 99.7% element rejection mean the plant permeate will have 99.7% rejection?

No. System results depend on feed composition, operating conditions, element age, staging, seals, blending and measurement method.

Can TDS alone determine whether RO or NF is suitable?

No. Ion composition, target quality, recovery, scaling and process constraints matter.

Is flux interchangeable with permeate capacity?

No. Flux is normalized by membrane area; capacity also depends on area, operating conditions and system configuration.
