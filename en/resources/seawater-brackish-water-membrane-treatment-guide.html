Seawater desalination / Buyer’s guide

Seawater RO: choose the process before the membrane

Seawater membrane performance is one input to system design. A purchase decision also needs seasonal feed quality, the required product-water specification, pretreatment, concentrate disposal and a defined energy boundary.

The decision in one minute

Choose a seawater membrane only after defining the feed envelope, the product-water specification and the concentrate outlet. Then compare complete configurations under the same conditions. The highest catalog flow is not automatically the best system.

Follow the water — and the concentrate

Pretreatment controls the risks presented by the intake; it is not an identical package for every coastal site. After SWRO, the intended use determines whether further desalination, stabilization or other treatment is required.

Engineering notes: conditions, mechanisms and limits

Start with a water report, not a membrane name

Provide salinity or measured conductivity with temperature, complete ionic analysis, boron, turbidity, SDI, organic and biological risks, intake type and seasonal extremes. Specify daily demand, peak flow, operating hours, destination and available power. Conductivity alone does not define scaling risk.

Choose the treatment train

Intake and screening → source-specific pretreatment → cartridge protection and verified dechlorination when required → high-pressure SWRO → product-specific polishing or second pass → stabilization/disinfection where required → storage and use. Manage concentrate and pretreatment residuals separately. This is a decision sequence, not a construction drawing.

Compare alternatives on the same basis

Compare lower-pressure and higher-rejection candidates at the same feed composition, temperature, membrane age, recovery and product specification. A lower test pressure or higher catalog flow does not establish lower whole-plant electricity use. Include pump efficiency, energy recovery where applicable, pretreatment and second-pass demand in the comparison.

Boron changes the selection question

Ask for feed boron, the product limit and the full seasonal temperature/pH envelope. Request an element-specific projection and determine whether a second pass or pH adjustment is needed. Do not infer boron compliance from salt rejection. Any pH change also needs a scaling assessment.

Recovery: define the boundary

System recovery is product flow divided by external feed flow. Element test recovery is not the plant target. For illustration only, 100 m³/h external feed at 44% recovery gives 44 m³/h product and 56 m³/h concentrate. With negligible salt passage, the ideal bulk concentration factor is 1/(1−0.44)=1.79; membrane-surface concentration and precipitation require a separate assessment.

Read the supplier case carefully

The supplied screenshots report a third-party seawater installation. Its capacity, membrane count and short commissioning results are not Yuchen project evidence or a design guarantee. Request original operating records and clarify whether each flow refers to one train or the complete plant.

Specify a reviewable quotation

Request the design envelope, predicted product quality, recovery boundary, pump/energy-recovery scope, materials, cleaning and monitoring provisions, consumables, acceptance procedure and concentrate plan. Final performance requires an agreed feed envelope and engineering review.

A balance check, not a recovery recommendation

Assume an illustrative external feed of 100 m³/h, a selected recovery of 40%, no recycle, steady flow and negligible salt passage.

Product = 100 × 0.40 = 40 m³/h
Concentrate = 100 − 40 = 60 m³/h
Ideal bulk concentration factor = 100 / 60 = 1.67

The water balance closes: 100 = 40 + 60. Salt conservation gives CfQf = CpQp + CcQc. The simplified factor assumes Cp ≈ 0; it does not predict membrane-surface concentration, precipitation or energy use.

Questions that change a purchase

Can I choose a seawater membrane from TDS alone?

No. TDS is only part of the feed envelope. Ion composition, temperature, intake variability, product limits and the concentrate outlet can change the suitable process.

Is a higher recovery always better?

No. It reduces concentrate volume for a fixed external feed but increases concentration. Check scaling, hydraulic limits, product quality and the complete operating cost.

Does high salt rejection guarantee boron compliance?

No. Require a boron-specific assessment at the relevant seasonal conditions and evaluate second-pass or other polishing needs.
