Red Sea power plant seawater RO case study

Case summary. A power plant in the Middle East uses Red Sea seawater as the source for a desalination train that supplies boiler makeup water. The project material provided for this case describes a site with two 660 MW generating units and four single-pass seawater reverse-osmosis (SWRO) systems.

Feed-water and treatment context

The supplied project description reports seawater total dissolved solids (TDS) of about 36,000 mg/L and elevated calcium and magnesium. The separate ion analysis totals 41,169.51 mg/L. VONTRON's published case gives source-water TDS of up to 41,170.14 mg/L, which is consistent with that ion-analysis total within rounding; the four-system operating table separately reports system feed TDS of 35,790–36,020 mg/L. These are different reported water points/records and should not be merged without confirming sample dates and measurement basis.

Reported system configuration

Each train is described as a single-pass arrangement with a 20 × 7 membrane array, a design permeate flow of at least 80 m³/h, and a stated recovery of at least 43%. The reported treatment train includes coagulation and sedimentation, multimedia filtration, activated carbon, a feed tank and supply pump, cartridge security filtration, a high-pressure pump, the SWRO stage, a permeate tank, a second-pass RO stage, ion exchange, and boiler makeup water.

Membrane element change and project reference

The customer-confirmed project information identifies the elements installed in Train 1 from 2019 as VONTRON SW8040HR-400. VONTRON's published Saudi Arabia power-plant case independently identifies four seawater desalination trains using 140 SW8040HR-400 elements per train and reports commissioning in 2019. This case is separate from the 2,000 L/h equipment configuration shown elsewhere on this product page, which lists four SW8040LE-400 elements.

Reported process train

Red Sea seawater intake → Coagulation and sedimentation → Multimedia filtration → Activated carbon → Feed-water tank and supply pump → Cartridge security filtration → High-pressure pump → Single-pass SWRO with SW8040HR-400 elements → Permeate tank → Second-pass RO → Ion exchange → Boiler makeup water

Reported operating result

The supplied comparison table records a snapshot for System 1 at 31.1 °C: feed flow 188.2 m³/h, permeate flow 81.2 m³/h, recovery 43.1%, feed TDS 36,020 mg/L, permeate TDS 228.8 mg/L, concentrate TDS 64,340 mg/L, and salt rejection 99.36%. The accompanying prose separately reports an initial rejection of 99.3% and output 10% above design. These figures represent different reported contexts and should remain separate unless the project owner confirms their measurement dates and definitions.

Published case result and customer-reported operating period

VONTRON's published case reports a 99.36% system desalination rate and says production and desalination performance met design values and remained stable during operation. The customer-provided records separately report nearly 2,000 m³ of seawater desalinated per day and stable permeate flow and salt rejection for more than two years during the documented operating period. The duration statement is historical and does not claim current operation.

How this case relates to the product shown on this page

This case illustrates a reported large industrial SWRO application and the importance of matching membrane elements to feed chemistry, pretreatment, pressure, recovery and permeate targets. Yuchen Water's 2,000 L/h product page documents a different equipment configuration using SW8040LE-400 elements; it is not the equipment described in this power-plant case.
